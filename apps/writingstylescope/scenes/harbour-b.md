# Notes from the quay

## Morning

Nobody on the quay expected the fog to lift before ten, so the market
opened late and the first buyers were the restaurant people from the town,
who want the best of the catch and will wait for it. Prices held steady on
whiting and fell a little on mackerel, of which there was more than anyone
could sell before the ice ran out.

## The long hull

Work on the long hull resumed the same morning. Three of the ribs had
opened along the grain over the winter and had to be steamed and refitted
before any planking could go on. The yard keeps a ledger of every rib by
number, so the replacements were cut to the old dimensions without anyone
having to measure the hull again, which saved most of a day.

## Gear

The new winch on the slipway draws more current than the old one, and the
fuse in the shed has gone twice under load. An electrician from the town
has been asked to look at the supply. Until then the winch is run only with
the shed lights off, which nobody likes on a dark afternoon.

## Visitors

A school party came down in the afternoon to see the lifeboat. The coxswain
showed them the station, let each of them sit at the wheel, and explained
why the boat is kept on a slipway rather than afloat: in a hurry it is
quicker to slide down than to row out to a mooring and climb aboard.
