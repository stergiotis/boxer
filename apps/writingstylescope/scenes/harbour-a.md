# Harbour log, spring

## Arrival

The ferry came round the breakwater a little after six, low in the water
with the first load of the season: timber for the boatyard, two crates of
seed potatoes and a mail sack that had been waiting at the railhead since
the thaw. The harbourmaster counted the crates twice and signed for the
sack once, which is the order he has done it in for eleven years.

## The boatyard

Work on the long hull resumed the same morning. Three of the ribs had
opened along the grain over the winter and had to be steamed and refitted
before any planking could go on. The yard keeps a ledger of every rib by
number, so the replacements were cut to the old dimensions without anyone
having to measure the hull again, which saved most of a day.

## Weather

A westerly set in by noon and held for four days. The fishing boats stayed
in, the nets were taken up to the loft to be mended, and the slipway was
used for nothing but hauling the pilot launch out to scrape its bottom. The
glass began to rise on the fifth morning and the boats were out before it
was properly light.

## Accounts

The harbour dues for the quarter came to a little under what was budgeted,
because two of the larger boats moved their moorings up the coast. The
committee agreed to hold the rate where it is for another year and to put
the difference towards the new fenders on the north quay, which have been
patched with old tyres long enough.
