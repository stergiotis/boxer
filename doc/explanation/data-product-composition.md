# Composable data products and their boundary

A recurring intuition in data-platform design: some data products are
chain-terminators — worth appending, but nothing composes after them —
and they feel like they sit "on the topological boundary" of the space of
products. This page gives that intuition a formal home. It names existing
mathematics rather than proving anything new, and the boundary language
is literal only in the three senses of §1.5; elsewhere the algebra and
the order structure do the work. Nothing on this page is implemented.
Chapter 1 is the formal picture; chapter 2 maps it onto data-mesh and
computer-science vocabulary.

## 1. The formal picture

### 1.1 Products, chains, composition

Fix a set of **interfaces** — output/input port schemas, or whatever
token decides compatibility. A **data product** p is an edge
in(p) → out(p) between interfaces; a **chain** is a non-empty directed
path, written in flow order: α ∘ p appends p downstream of α, and is
defined when α's final interface equals in(p). Chains under
concatenation form the free category on this graph (minus identities —
empty chains add nothing below). Associativity comes for free; nothing
else is assumed.

The building blocks A, B, C are families of products, and they force the
one genuine modelling decision in this picture: at which granularity does
composability live?

- **Type-level** — whether a ∘ b is defined depends only on the
  families; composability is a relation on {A, B, C}. Sealing (§1.2) is
  then a property of a whole building block: every c ∈ C seals, or none
  does.
- **Port-level** — every product carries its own port schemas; the
  family letters are labels, and sealing is a per-product property.

Everything below works for either. The choice decides whether "null"
names a fourth building block or a predicate cutting across the three.

Deferred: products with several inputs (joins) turn chains into trees
and the free category into a free operad; the notions below transfer at
notational cost. This page stays single-input.

### 1.2 Sealing, structural and economic

Let U assign a real **utility** to every chain — positive, zero, or
negative.

- A chain α is **sealed** when no product extends it — equivalently,
  its final interface is a **sink**: no product consumes it.
- A product z is **sealing** when out(z) is a sink, so every chain
  ending in z is sealed.
- A product z is **null** when it is sealing and its marginal utility is
  non-negative: U(α ∘ z) − U(α) ≥ 0 wherever defined. (The absolute
  reading — U(α ∘ z) ≥ 0 — is the same test against a zero baseline;
  marginal is the more informative primitive.)

A second, finer notion is invisible to the port graph. Let
L = {α : U(α) > 0}, the valuable chains. Call α **economically sealed**
when no strict extension of α lies in L: growth may still type-check,
but is never again valuable. Structural sealing is the special case
where extensions do not even exist. A platform needs both notions — the
first is readable off the catalog, the second only off U.

### 1.3 The null is canonical

Three constructions say "all sealed things are one thing", and they
agree.

**Adjoin a zero.** Make composition total by sending every undefined
composite to a fresh absorbing element 0. The sealed chains together
with 0 then form a two-sided ideal — prefixing a sealed chain leaves it
sealed, extending one gives 0 — and it is exactly the ideal generated by
the sealing products. The Rees quotient collapses this ideal to a single
point: the algebra of still-growing chains plus one canonical null.
Adjoining a zero is a universal construction (left adjoint to forgetting
the zero), so there is essentially one way to add a null element.

**Merge the sinks.** No edge leaves a sink, so identifying all sink
interfaces into a single interface ⊤ ("consumed") creates no new
composite and changes no utility. Without loss of generality a platform
has exactly one terminal interface, and the null products are exactly
the products into ⊤.

**Minimize the automaton.** Myhill–Nerode: call α ≈ β when exactly the
same continuations work for both — α ∘ γ ∈ L ⇔ β ∘ γ ∈ L for every γ,
the empty continuation included. The quotient is the minimal automaton
recognizing L; it is finite whenever utility depends on boundedly much
state (for instance, on the type sequence alone). Economically sealed
chains occupy the states with no valuable future, and those split in
two: **dead** (not in L, no extension reaches L) and
**accepting-but-futureless** (in L, nothing more to gain). The dead
state is the canonical economic null; the accepting-futureless states
are where value has been realized. This dead-versus-realizing split
recurs in §1.4.

### 1.4 Value is a boundary quantity

Define value-to-go V(α) = sup{U(β) : β = α or β extends α}, assuming
the sup exists (utility bounded along extensions, or discounted). V
satisfies the Bellman recursion

    V(α) = max( U(α), max over composable p of V(α ∘ p) )

and the recursion bottoms out on sealed chains: **V = U exactly on the
boundary**. Interior values are determined by boundary values — the
max-plus analogue of a harmonic function determined by its Dirichlet
data. Replacing max with expectation under any stochastic growth process
makes the analogy exact and classical: on an absorbing Markov chain,
expected terminal utility is harmonic off the absorbing states and
equals U on them (unique when absorption is almost sure; the Martin
boundary is the refinement for mass that never seals).

Two derived quantities carry the governance content:

- **Option value** O(α) = V(α) − U(α) ≥ 0 — what continuing is still
  worth. Appending a null product realizes value and sets O to zero.
- **Sealing regret** V(α) − U(α ∘ z) ≥ 0 — what appending this
  particular z, here, forfeits.

Sealing is therefore not a defect: it is where the utility the platform
exists for actually exits. The defects are premature sealing (large
regret) and the dead stratum (sealing at non-positive utility).

### 1.5 Where "boundary" is literal

A finite discrete set of products carries no useful topology; stated
over the products alone, "null products live on the boundary" is a
metaphor. It becomes a theorem in three settings, each putting the
topology where the structure actually is — on chains, or on limits of
products.

**(a) The chain domain.** Chains under the prefix order, completed with
their infinite limits, form a domain whose maximal points are the sealed
finite chains together with the infinite chains — everything that cannot
grow. With finitely many products the extension tree is finitely
branching and this maximal set is closed in the Lawson topology: a
genuine topological "edge of the space". Within it, every sealed finite
chain is an isolated point (the cone above it meets the maximal set in
itself alone), while an infinite chain is a limit point precisely when
each of its prefixes has some other maximal completion. The boundary
thus decomposes into a discrete stratum — the nulls — and a limit
stratum of perpetual growth.

**(b) The continuum of products.** When products come in parametrized
families — θ ∈ Θ ⊆ ℝⁿ: schema parameters, quality scores, freshness
targets — composability becomes a consumer correspondence C(θ) ⊆ Θ, and
the extendable region is E = {θ : C(θ) ≠ ∅}. Sealing products are the
complement of E, and a null product that is a limit of composable ones
lies on the topological frontier ∂E, where the consumer set degenerates
to empty. This is the literal reading of the opening intuition; the
discrete case is its degenerate shadow, which is why the intuition
resists formalization there.

**(c) The absorbing boundary.** Give chain growth any dynamics — even a
uniform random walk on the port graph. Sealed states are absorbing, and
§1.4 is the associated potential theory. "Absorbing boundary", in the
random-walk and PDE sense, is the phrase the intuition reaches for: not
the frontier of a point set, but the states where a process stops and
value is read off.

## 2. The same picture in platform terms

### 2.1 The data-mesh reading

The formalism forces a trichotomy — no in-mesh producer, interior, no
in-mesh consumer — and it is the data-mesh product classification:
source-aligned ("atomic"), aggregate ("composite"), consumer-aligned
("user-facing").

| Formal object | Data-mesh reading |
| --- | --- |
| interface (vertex) | output-port schema; the data contract |
| product (edge) | data product |
| chain (path) | lineage path through the mesh |
| source — no in-mesh producer | source-aligned / atomic product, fed by an operational system |
| interior product | aggregate / composite product |
| sealing product — sink output | consumer-aligned / user-facing product: dashboard, report, served model, export |
| ⊤, the merged sink | consumption: where value leaves the mesh |
| U | realized value of a lineage path |
| marginal utility of z | the product's incremental contribution |
| null product | user-facing product worth (weakly) adding; realizes value, ends growth |
| economically sealed chain | still typable onward, never again valuable |
| option value O | what the mesh's remaining growth is worth here |
| sealing regret | value forfeited by consuming here rather than growing on |

Three consequences for platform governance:

- The failure modes worth instrumenting are premature sealing — a
  user-facing product built where option value was still large — and the
  dead stratum: sinks realizing non-positive utility, the natural
  deprecation candidates.
- Structural sealing is decidable from the catalog alone (an output port
  no product consumes). Economic sealing needs U — lineage and usage
  observed over time. A platform that records both as queryable facts
  (boxer's data-centric wager; see [why-boxer](./why-boxer.md)) can
  evaluate the dead-state test as a query.
- The granularity decision of §1.1 is what a mesh settles when it
  standardizes contracts: with type-level contracts, "user-facing" is a
  kind of product; with port-level contracts it is a role a product
  turns out to play.

### 2.2 The computer-science reading

| Formal object | Computer-science reading |
| --- | --- |
| free category on the port graph | typed composition; a chain is a well-typed point-free pipeline |
| adjoined 0 | absorbing zero of a semigroup; the trap state of an automaton |
| sealed ideal, Rees quotient | "once dead, always dead", turned into a quotient algebra |
| Myhill–Nerode quotient | minimal DFA of the valuable-chain language; the weighted variant keeps magnitudes |
| dead vs accepting-but-futureless | reject trap vs accept states with no live continuation |
| V and its Bellman recursion | dynamic programming; max-plus (tropical) linear algebra over the port graph |
| expectation variant | absorbing Markov chains; the Dirichlet problem on a graph |
| chain domain and its maximal points | domain theory: finite words vs streams, Scott/Lawson topology |

Three of these deserve prose:

- **Transformations vs actions.** Dataflow engines draw exactly this
  boundary: transformations compose lazily (interior), actions realize a
  value and end composition (boundary). A mesh's user-facing products
  are its actions.
- **The answer type.** In continuation-passing style every computation
  composes toward one fixed answer type; ⊤ is that type. Pure functional
  programs likewise compose effects symbolically until the runtime
  executes them at the program's edge — value realization as sealing.
- **Dead-state elimination.** The economic null is what automaton
  minimization calls the dead state. Detecting economically sealed
  products over observed lineage is dead-state detection, and
  deprecating the dead stratum is the platform analogue of dead-code
  elimination.
