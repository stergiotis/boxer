# Changelog: 2026-09-13 – 2026-09-30 (feature commits)

Compiles the `feat` commits of the window: 166 of 574 commits. Fixes,
refactors, docs and chores are out of scope — this entry answers "what can the
codebase do now that it couldn't a fortnight ago", not "what changed". Three
exceptions are made and marked where they occur: `fix`, `refactor`, `perf`,
`chore` and `docs` commits are cited where they close, reverse or supersede an
arc that would otherwise read as unfinished, or where an ADR arrived with one;
the ADR-0267 widget migration gets a section of its own although it is entirely
`refactor`, because it carries the window's three marked breaking changes; and
the documentation and ADR housekeeping are summarised at the end. ADR statuses
were read on 2026-10-01.

## The window in brief

This section carries no commit hashes and no links; everything it claims is
cited in the body below.

A tag was cut eighteen commits into the window, so almost nothing here has
shipped under a version: five of the 166 features are in v0.0.23, and the rest
is unreleased.

Three through-lines. The first is graphs as analysis rather than drawing: an
in-process graph engine, a neighbour graph and HDBSCAN replacing play's UMAP
fit, a way to read a clustering back off its matrix as rules, and a columnar
declaration so play's graph data stops changing shape four times per rebuild.
The second is the bus as the one route out of an app. Introspection reads, model
inference, HTTP egress and an app's own files each become a declared, audited
request rather than something a window reached on its own. The capability gate
had reported each of those direct reaches as drift, and the operator windows
built in the window (app center, app state, watchbill) are the first consumers.
The third is the imzero2 platform: one contract for every Go widget, applied
across the tree, plus the UI in a browser tab, a native Windows viewer and
scenes as single runnable documents.

Beside those: vector fields on the map with a time strip, ad-hoc datasets over
kernel-freed sealed files, Chat and Cards panes in play, scored renderings,
road routing, a guided ClickHouse sync and an ingestion host for streaming
frameworks.

The window costs a reader more than the previous one did. Three commits carry
the breaking marker, all from the widget migration, and about a dozen more break
an interface, a wire or an environment variable without it. Two ADR
renumberings mean commit subjects cite numbers that now name other decisions.
Forty-one ADRs were added; nine of them are still proposed.

## analytics/graph: an in-process graph engine (ADR-0229)

[ADR-0229](../adr/0229-graph-analytics-engine.md) (accepted, `7e2b623b`,
written as 0226 and renumbered in `1240dce9` — see the renumbering note above)
puts graph algorithms in process rather than in a library the widget would
call per frame. `e9f1a1e5` is three packages. `csr` maps ids to dense slots in
id order and builds forward and reverse adjacency by two stable counting
passes, so rows come out sorted; parallel edges collapse with summed weights,
self-loops are kept and counted, and a topology fingerprint ignores weights.
`engine` is Ligra's shape — a vertex subset in sparse or dense form, an edge
map whose direction is an argument and whose sweep switches between serial
push and parallel pull on the Ligra threshold, and a fixed-chunk reduction
whose fold order does not depend on the worker count. `algo` carries degrees,
BFS with deterministic parents, union-find components, iterative Tarjan SCC,
pull PageRank, k-core, triangle counting, Brandes betweenness (exact or by
seeded pivots) and Bron–Kerbosch cliques with an output cap; every call takes
a context and returns a truncation flag naming the limit that stopped it.
Results are checked against a brute-force oracle and gonum/graph, and one
worker against many bit for bit on an R-MAT graph.

`cfae6d34` gives the engine a vocabulary (ADR-0232 §SD6, §SD7), because play,
the facts follow-up and the navigation layer would otherwise each have spelled
their own list: `algo.MetricE` names sixteen per-vertex metrics with a parser,
a `Kind` — ordinal or categorical, which decides whether a metric can size a
node or only group it — a symmetric reading and a seeded flag, and `Compute`
returns a slot-aligned column. NaN, not zero, is "no value for this slot", so
an unreached vertex does not land at the bottom of a ramp.
`PageRankOptions.Teleport` adds personalised PageRank, which enters the
vocabulary as `relevance`; the uniform path keeps its exact arithmetic, so a
classic rank is unchanged bit for bit. `68e7ecbc` is the review pass over the
engine, HDBSCAN and knn below.

## Projection as a neighbour graph (ADR-0230)

[ADR-0230](../adr/0230-neighbour-graph-and-neighbour-embedding-force-model.md)
(accepted, `d4d1497b`, with a Graphistry analysis behind it; written as 0227
and renumbered in `1240dce9`) replaces play's UMAP fit with the pieces UMAP is
made of, drawn by graphview. `3676b67a` is the neighbour-graph producer:
exact brute-force k-NN, umap-learn's smooth-kNN bandwidth search, membership
weights and the fuzzy union, with golden fixtures written once from umap-go's
exported stages; ten thousand rows of sixteen features build in about half a
second on eight threads. `b0fcf968` adds HDBSCAN over the distance-weighted
graph — mutual reachability, a Kruskal dendrogram, condensation by minimum
cluster size, selection by excess of mass — returning a label, a membership
probability and a stability; over a neighbour graph a bridge outlier is a
low-probability member rather than noise, and the probability is the reading.

`96eb9660` gives graphview a second force model on the same Barnes–Hut tree:
`ForceModelNeighborEmbedding` is the t-SNE kernel with one knob,
`Exaggeration` (about 1 draws t-SNE, 4 UMAP, 30 ForceAtlas2), under a
geometric annealing schedule. The zero value of `ForceParams.Model` is the
Fruchterman–Reingold step unchanged to the bit. `Options.HideEdges` keeps the
edges for the layout and paints none, since a neighbour graph's edges are its
input rather than a reading. A test reproduces the trade-off Böhm, Berens and
Kobak report: neighbour recall falls and distance correlation rises as the
exaggeration grows.

`b1619e26` is play's Projection tab rebuilt on these: the goroutine runs
`knn.Build` and `algo.HDBSCAN` and computes no coordinate; the render thread
declares a node per row, the cluster as an aura for members above a
probability floor, and lets the force model lay it out. `card.RunUMAP` and its
options are removed in the same commit (see breaking changes).
`2ab3fb1c` then declares the neighbour graph as columns rather than rows, which
had been 16% of CPU in a settled tab.

## Explaining a clustering (ADR-0235) and feature sets (ADR-0238)

[ADR-0235](../adr/0235-explaining-a-clustering-threshold-tree-and-rank-contrasts.md)
(accepted, `a1c17b40`, `a060e761`) asks what a cluster *is*, read back off the
matrix that produced it. `84907233` adds `analytics/explain`: a CART threshold
tree with splits at the shortest decimal between adjacent values, so a rule
reads and round-trips as `x > 37`, read at any depth instead of refitted, with
one-vs-rest fits and rules spelled as SQL; per-feature contrasts by the
Mann–Whitney AUC, which is indifferent to the monotone transforms the
clustering ran under; and, over binary items, lift, weighted relative accuracy
and a Bonferroni-corrected Fisher exact test, with a deterministic beam search
for the best short conjunction. `ee129de9` adds the items: `card.ItemExtractor`
turns an entity into the facts it holds — tagged sections, co-groups, short
tagged values, low-cardinality memberships — and leaves identity and content
out. `cf108a0d` puts a "why these clusters" section under the Projection tab:
per cluster, a SQL predicate with its precision and recall, the features that
set it apart, and a "by attributes" reading whose predicate runs against the
result as it is.

[ADR-0238](../adr/0238-projection-feature-sets-hashed-structural-identity.md)
(accepted, `29689e31`) makes the matrix a per-run choice. `43cfc05a` adds
`card.StructureMatrix`, a signed feature hash of an entity's structural items —
sections, co-groups, memberships, never values — so two entities of the same
shape get the same row whatever their values; items now carry the column
handle a person would write. `e01a116b` offers shape, structure, or components
(one column per registered component kind) in the tab, spells the attribute
predicates over handles and `LW_COMPONENT_FILTER` rather than physical names,
and adds "publish as dataset", which writes the run and its rules as
`keelson('projection')` and `keelson('projection_rules')` through the ad-hoc
store.

## play's graph contract (ADR-0231) over a columnar graphview (ADR-0232)

[ADR-0232](../adr/0232-play-first-class-consumer-columnar-graphview-engine-vocabulary.md)
(accepted at `45375dcd`) starts from one dataset that changed shape four times
per rebuild — Arrow columns, a row-shaped model, a spec per row, and the
widget reconciling the rows back into its own arrays. `cead9c40` gives
graphview a columnar declaration (`NodeColumns`, `EdgeColumns`,
`RenderColumns`): one optional slice per spec field, ragged columns in Arrow's
list layout, and NaN as the unset value so a declared zero is a zero — a zero
strength pulls nothing, a zero opacity paints nothing. The row and column forms
paint a byte-identical stream. Slots follow declaration order, so a metric
column can index the widget's arrays directly. `09831a52` makes play's graph
model columns sorted by interned id — the CSR's slot order and therefore the
widget's — and the two middle shapes go. Four `perf` commits followed in the
widget (`d1439be3`, `f0be2e7b`, `4e81f0b0`, `a992eb20`): the Barnes–Hut walk
over linearised leaves (37% of CPU with the layout running, 1.5–1.7× on the
package benchmarks) and the aura overlay (28%, 1.6×).

[ADR-0231](../adr/0231-play-graph-contract-widening.md) (proposed in
`576c8836`, accepted at `45375dcd`) widens what a query can say to the graph
panels. Seam A (`17406e16`) adds per-row columns — opacity, pick, selected,
radius, pins, `lat`/`lon`, start positions, the pull family, `groups`, edge
`id`, `length`, `strength` — each claimed on name *and* type, with NULL as "not
declared for this row". `lat`/`lon` are pins projected in Web Mercator, not a
map. Seam B (`5b3ca248`) is the `graph_opts` CTE, a one-row settings table for
properties of the whole drawing; columns rather than keys, because a column
name is schema-visible to completion and the claim, where a misspelt key is
silently ignored. Nothing it can say blanks a drawing: a value outside the
vocabulary is refused in the status line and the default stays. The query sets
the default, an explicit chrome setting overrides it, and an *auto* position
hands the channel back. `a89f2569` adds the gesture seam — state read off the
widget each frame, moments from the event queue — one reserved-signal table in
place of four registrations, and metric selectors that spend `algo.MetricE`, so
play keeps no table of its own. `9250233f` is the review pass, including seeded
selectors that follow a click without a rebuild.

## graphview and icicle: legibility

`81d0792f` fixes a scale mismatch in graphview: node radii are world units and
shrank with the zoom while arrow heads and strokes held their screen size, so
a forty-node tree at fit read as two-pixel dots under ten-pixel heads. A disc
now holds a screen floor, the screen-sized decorations follow the zoom below 1,
labels paint over a halo and clear the stroke, and the default style takes
implot's surface and accent roles. `ce4843be` makes a non-nil empty column read
as absent.

`31837e5f` makes colour-by-branch the icicle's default: the by-label hash gave
a child a colour unrelated to its parent's, so one call path alternated yellow
and deep red. A subtree now stays in one family of the Lajolla band; by-label
and by-depth remain as options.

## watchbill: a client protocol (ADR-0234), worker presence (ADR-0237), a management window (ADR-0236)

The previous window left watchbill as a store and a worker that only its own
host could feed. [ADR-0234](../adr/0234-watchbill-client-protocol-and-worker-presence.md)
(accepted, `55e2d595`) gives apps a way in. `02b6c395` adds the wire:
`watchbillRequest` carries five verbs — enqueue, cancel, retry, get, list —
and `watchbillReply` answers with the jobs as parallel columns, on generated
codecs and a new membership cohort (ordinals 156–191). `9ce3f758` has the
worker serve it on `watchbill.job.<op>`, attributing an enqueue to the
envelope's sender. `Client` is the typed face, and `WithArgs`/`ArgsOf` keep a
job's args a generated DTO by construction. `StoreI.List` takes a filter,
`Config.Queues` narrows what a worker drains (nil still drains every queue),
`Submit` is enqueue plus wake, and `keelson('watchbill_worker')` reports the
worker's status. The CLI worker now stands a run the way the host does —
start row, heartbeats, liveness — so a host worker on the same cell no longer
sweeps the CLI worker's running jobs as orphaned. `a43f0709` is the first app
client: `watchbilldemo` registers a handler for its own kind, enqueues
through the client, lists the queue with a cancel and a retry per row, and
runs no bus request on the frame goroutine.

[ADR-0237](../adr/0237-watchbill-worker-presence.md) (accepted) closes a
deferral ADR-0234 recorded: a worker states what it drains (`1f134afe`). A
`watchbillWorker` kind on the runtime vocabulary is written as an appended
fact — started after the worker subscribes, stopped when it stops cleanly —
while liveness stays the runtime heartbeat. `FoldWorkers` folds the rows per
run by the sweep's own rule, so `keelson('watchbill_worker')` lists every run
seen on the cell, not only this process's.

[ADR-0236](../adr/0236-watchbill-management-app.md) (accepted) is the
operator's view (`64fb1b29`): the list verb's rows under state pills with a
kind filter, a table in the shape of play's Table pane, a split that a window
resize does not move, and a detail pane with the state-machine chip, policy,
the last error, the trail, the worker row and the task monitor. Cancel and
retry go through the client. A launch config (`watchbillLaunch`) lets another
app open the window on one job. `c541930a` adds watchbill to the capability
inspector as its seventh capability, with a schema block built from the
store's own manipulator.

A review pass over the worker and store followed, cited because it changes
what the claim guarantees rather than how it reads: a stopped worker leaves
its runs for the sweep (`d967fc03`), the sweep cancels a dead run's
cancel-requested job (`49784dd6`), `SqlStore` refuses a duplicate job id as
`MemStore` does (`f490b7cf`), rows held with no run in flight settle
(`6e1a5df4`), timeout and backoff durations saturate (`77414ab4`), the list
verb returns the newest N rather than the oldest (`4d62b1e7`), and a failure
under a bus is no longer read as a cancel (`5f710ad0`). `504539fe` states the
claim's guarantees in Jepsen's terms, and `6b577132` adds the
[architecture page](../explanation/watchbill-architecture.md).

## App state: one state table, and a manager for it (ADR-0185)

[ADR-0185](../adr/0185-durable-app-state-manager.md) was carried as proposed
with no code through two entries; it is now **accepted** (`a505bee2`) and
built. The substrate came first. `5aa787d9` carries out the P1–P5 items of
ADR-0105's 2026-08-15 update: persisted values, workingsets and column-width
overrides share one generated store on `boxer.persiststate`, on the rule that
a trail goes to `boxer.facts` and state to `boxer.persiststate`. Every live row
carries an `Owner` component (app, run, window), because the generator
refuses two kinds naming one membership; keys are kind-prefixed and escape
their non-final segments, since app ids are import paths and nest, and an
unescaped per-app prefix would take in every applet. hostboot opens the state
store whether or not the persist service runs, so a host without
`runtime.persist` keeps workingsets and widths. `79d0436c` gives the
generated state view `ScanLive<Kind>` — the newest row per key in one
statement, collapsed before the component test, because a tombstone carries no
component and testing first would return the row it superseded — and a
key-prefix range on every scan verb.

The milestones then run in order. M0 (`a38ccc3a`) is `keelson('app_state')`,
one row per live entry of any kind from one scan of the `Owner` component:
the payload's size but never its bytes, and an unknown kind shown as
`unknown` rather than skipped. M1 (`55ec3188`) is an applet book over it,
browsable before any manager existed. M2 (`b34d9b0c`) is the delete seam,
`runtime.appstate.{delete,forget}`: delete must address a live entry of the
named app and kind or it is refused, forget attempts every entry and reports
per kind what was cleared and what failed, and every call — refused ones
included — is audited. M3 (`0e4a4a99`) is the App state window: apps on the
left with entry count and size, entries in the middle, Delete per entry and a
Forget per app that is armed first behind a banner naming the app and the
count.

## keelson.query: introspection reads as a bus capability (ADR-0253)

Until this window, a window that wanted a `keelson()` table read it from the
local query endpoint over loopback HTTP. That read was invisible to the
capability model — undeclared, unprompted, audited under the runner's
identity — and the capslock gate reported it as `CAPABILITY_NETWORK` drift
for the watchbill and app-state windows.
[ADR-0253](../adr/0253-introspection-table-reads-as-a-bus-capability.md)
(accepted, `ae3a3154`) makes the read a request on the bus.

`25f4d750` is the wire: a request carrying table, statement and FORMAT, and a
reply carrying the body bytes in that format, undecoded, so a JSONEachRow
reader and an ArrowStream consumer share one reply. `3b687a98` is the family,
`keelson.query.<table>`, one sticky publish grant per table, served over the
ADR-0094 §SD4 in-process engine — its first production caller. The gate holds
a statement to the granted table before the engine sees it, default-deny:
macros expanded first, provably read-only, parsed and scope-resolved, and a
second table, a qualified name or a table function each refused by kind.
Unparseable SQL is refused rather than run, because the engine's fallback for
it is to snapshot every registered table. `c05b91c7` moves both windows onto
it, and both capslock findings go. `e296f251` puts `{name:Type}` parameters
on the wire and has the HTTP `/query` endpoint answer over the same engine,
with a split rewrite that sends an ordinary table to an in-process snapshot
and a sealed dataset to this server's `/table` route, the one place it is
decrypted. `534c9308` registers the family in the capability inspector.
Later in the window `90ce3eb5` replaces the Go reader `Rows[T]` with
`Columns`, decoding ArrowStream through `chrows`, so integer width no longer
depends on a JSON quoting setting; recorded as a dated update on the ADR.

## The app center (ADR-0260)

[ADR-0260](../adr/0260-app-center-one-page-per-app.md) (**proposed**,
`6ce79915`) assembles one page per registered app from the introspection
tables that name it (`8e2971a3`): runs, logs and audited requests across
processes, this process's events, declared and held capabilities, kept state,
coverage of its package subtree, the ADRs its code cites, and its jobs,
datasets, model calls and tasks — one `keelson.query` read per lens, one
sticky grant per table. The launcher's detail pane gains Inspect, which opens
it on an app. The cross-run tables are new (`164a357a`): `app_runs`,
`app_logs` and `app_audit` read the facts trail over every process, bounded
by a 30-day look-back and a cap because a provider sees no predicate.
`617edc20` ends an unclosed session at its process's last heartbeat and
leaves out lifecycle rows that name no run, since those predate ADR-0191 and
pairing them folded every such session of an app into one. `b6a0e80c` gives
every section an Open in play and puts the Runs section on play's Timeline,
one bar per window session.

## One render goroutine, checked and measured (ADR-0261)

[ADR-0261](../adr/0261-one-render-goroutine-for-every-app.md) (**proposed**,
`89d0b7eb`) writes down a rule every app already relied on: a host draws all
its windows from one loop, the FFFI channel has no lock and the frame state
is process-global, so `Mount`, `Frame`, `Unmount` and every imzero2 call run
on the goroutine running `application.Run`. What was missing was
visibility — when one app's `Frame` is slow, every window waits, and nothing
said which app. `da6586f3` adds `Fffi2.BindToCurrentGoroutine`, after which a
send, capture or receive from another goroutine panics with a stack naming
the caller, and a message counter. The check reads the goroutine id from the
stack header, so it is opt-in: `IMZERO2_RENDER_GOROUTINE_CHECK` (`0ab962ee`),
off by default. `cd2a060c` times each window's `Mount` and `Frame` and the

## Ad-hoc datasets v2: a sealed file, an owned record, a platform follower (ADR-0240)

[ADR-0240](../adr/0240-adhoc-datasets-v2-sealed-store-owned-capability.md)
(proposed in `20dd946b`, accepted at `1581a25b`) replaces the mechanisms of
[ADR-0134](../adr/0134-adhoc-datasets.md), which is now **superseded** — its
requirements are kept, its custody model is not. M1 (`0c5fc30d`) adds
`sealed.File`: an `O_TMPFILE` inode with no directory entry, freed by the kernel
on last close (a crash included), holding its own key, with one linear writer at
creation, concurrent seekable readers afterwards, a reader count, and `Retire`
that closes at the last reader or a ceiling. The reader authenticates the final
chunk at open, which closes a hole in the v1 reader where a header followed by
an empty chunk read as a valid empty stream. tally's recording stage moves onto
it and drops its path, key slice, rename and remove.

M2 and M3 landed together (`41feb453`). The registry entry *is* the record —
alias, owner, stats and the sealed file under one lock — and custody is
ownership of the file object, so no operation resolves a key by name. The
named-file store, adhocdata's copy of the AEAD format, `introspect.EncryptedEntry`,
the chlocal broker's key store and decrypt path, and `adhoc.grant` are gone.
Publish streams decoded batches straight into the sealed writer and re-admits
against live state at commit, so a republish racing a retract cannot drift the
quota. A republish or retract by anyone but the publishing instance is
`ErrNotOwner`, unless the dataset was published `KeepAfterClose`, which makes
it the app's. M4 (`73c8d158`) ties a dataset's life to the window that
published it: the bus announces `runtime.instance.closed` — the first member of
the lifecycle family ADR-0026 §SD3 reserved — and the service retracts what that
instance published, so three publishers stop retracting in `Unmount` and lose a
blocking bus round-trip on the mount thread. The lifecycle test found that
`KeepAfterClose` had been dropped on the wire and never reached the service.

M5 (`466ff9ee`) lifts the applet host's dataset binder into the platform as
`adhocdata.Follower` (events as hints, resolve as truth, a reconcile tick) and
adds `adhocdata.Publisher`, one app's side of one dataset; five apps drop their
handle bookkeeping for it, and the fixture lab stops minting two datasets per
click against the quotas. M6 has two halves: `d201c775` gives `PlayLaunch` a
`Datasets` field, so a launched play window follows aliases the way a declared
applet does and publishers name an alias instead of splicing a handle into
seeded SQL — a re-capture reaches the open window and the buffer stays
pasteable; `6d3a906a` moves the `adhoc.*` wire from hand-rolled CBOR onto
generated leeway codecs (one request kind, one reply kind, two announcements),
the shape the sibling brokers already use.

Five `fix` commits close the hardening this arc needed and are cited for that
reason: `8379bcc0` bounds what a published stream decodes to, since a few
megabytes of ZSTD could otherwise expand to gigabytes before the quota check
ran; `3b71044f` counts the stream's own bodies apart in that budget; `73a036fb`
opens a reader under the record's lock; `88efde4d` keeps a follower from binding
a retracted handle; and `b28f873d` escapes backslashes in structure column
names. The how-to is [adhoc-datasets](../howto/adhoc-datasets.md).

## play: the Cards pane and row-value glosses (ADR-0245)

[ADR-0245](../adr/0245-play-cards-panel-and-row-value-glosses.md) (accepted,

## imzero2: one contract for Go widgets (ADR-0267)

Cited under its own heading although the migration is entirely `refactor`,
because the arc carries all three of the window's marked breaking changes and
the one `feat` in it (`d5f4ef98`) only makes sense as its first step.

[ADR-0267](../adr/0267-imzero2-go-widget-api-contract-immediate-and-semi-retained.md)
(accepted at `810e6470`, with a
[style survey](../adr-background-work/imzero2-go-widget-api-styles-survey.md)
in `5a0fe920`) starts from a census: 49 Go widgets above the bindings in eight
shapes, with eleven ways to receive an id space, seven option mechanisms and
twelve ways to report what the user did. Two of the inconsistencies were bugs
rather than taste. A layout probe keyed on `c.ProbeSeq(scopeKey, role)` ignores
the id stack, so two instances whose hosts picked the same scope key shared one
slot; and a widget building absolute ids from a caller's `idPrefix` could not
be nested twice and had nowhere but a process-global map to keep state. The
decision is that a widget is one of three shapes chosen by one test — **fluid**
(one binding, no state), **immediate-mode** (`Render(Input) Result` with
host-owned `State`), or **semi-retained** (`New(ids, scopeKey, Options)`, a
public `Opts` re-read every frame, `Render` returning `Events`) — with numbered
rules W1–W20 a checker can cite. The ADR records that the drift had already
stopped: everything written since August took one of the two non-trivial
shapes, and what was missing was the sentence saying *only these two*.

M0 is `d5f4ef98`: `WidgetIdStack.ProbeSeq` hashes the role into the current
stack-derived id, replacing five hand-rolled `probeSalt` copies (W7).
`9e6e40b1` (M1–M4) moves the widgets, their callers in apps, the demo
registry, the runtime and the host, and adds `widgets/conformance` — ten
syntactic checkers over every package with an allowlist of the twelve packages
still to migrate, which may only shrink; every migrated package gained a
headless one-frame test. `404b534e` (M5) moves treemap, timeline, markdown,
leewaywidgets, portolan and timescrubber and empties the allowlist, and
`7c646cbe` (M6) deletes the `Events()` accessors M1 had kept for one phase.
Three refinements made while applying the rules are part of the accepted
decision: a render entry that validates its declaration may return
`(Events, error)` (W3), W14 binds only goroutines a widget's own methods
start, and a painter helper's knobs are a `Style` embedded in its `Input`. The
authoring reference is the imzero2 skill's §21; the ADR keeps the *why*.

## imzero2 remote viewing: session and stream lifetimes (ADR-0242), a native Windows viewer (ADR-0243)

[ADR-0242](../adr/0242-imzero2-session-and-stream-lifetimes.md) (accepted;
cited although its commit is a `fix`, since it is what the viewer below is
written against) is recorded with `e545aa42`, which repairs the headless host's
session handling: authoritative state — the role roster, stream hellos — is no
longer disposable frame traffic and survives backpressure; input belongs to an
owner and a focus interval, so losing focus cancels held input rather than
completing a click, and disconnect cancellation is server-side; and encoder
supervision and viewer joining progress on keyframe delivery rather than on
pixel changes, so a viewer joining an idle application is not left waiting.
Origin, authentication and TLS policy are unchanged.

[ADR-0243](../adr/0243-imzero2-native-windows-viewer.md) (accepted) adds a
standalone Rust Win32 client against the same protobuf wire (`8bf37a05`):
native controls for connection and settings, a separate D3D11 child window for
video, in-process FFmpeg decoding of H.264, VP9 and AV1 with D3D11VA verified
against the actual stream and an explicit, reported software fallback, input
forwarding under ADR-0242's focus contract, and a Linux-to-Windows MinGW build
with portable packaging and Wine smoke checks. It depends on no part of the
imzero2 host crate. `33c496eb` reverses SD1's "validation remains enabled" for
one case it had deferred: a self-signed or private host could not be reached
at all, so the viewer gains a SHA-256 certificate pin (`--pin-sha256`) and an
`--insecure` skip that still verifies handshake signatures and says so in the
title bar for the whole session, since this client sends keystrokes and
clipboard. Web-roots verification stays the default.

Three `fix` commits close what the first cut got wrong, each measured under
Wine: `6ac9621e` — every `wss://` connect panicked inside the network worker
because no rustls crypto provider was chosen, and a failed connect said only
"transport/TLS/handshake"; `4e428d29` — with the display powered off, presents
at sync interval 1 blocked on vblanks that never came and the session rejoined
in a loop (2 frames and 9 rejoins before, 120 of 120 frames after); and
`ebdde0f1` — SD4's "rejoin on overflow" had been built as instantaneous, so a
burst of about sixteen messages ended the session, where a full queue is now
waited on and only a consumer stuck for seconds rejoins.

On the host side, `6c106b2a` converts BGRA to NV12 on the GPU, which had been
about as expensive on the CPU as rasterising the frame — but only where a
once-per-process probe shows the VA driver converts to BT.709, because the
driver rather than the filter's options picks the matrix. The CPU path now
declares limited-range BT.601 in the stream instead of leaving decoders to
guess.

## Vector fields on the map (ADR-0249) and in play (ADR-0250)

[ADR-0249](../adr/0249-vector-fields-on-the-map-particles-over-a-batched-segment-opcode.md)
(accepted, `8439df3a`, with a clean-room
[survey](../adr-background-work/vector-field-flow-visualization-survey.md) of
particle renderers and the defects they shipped) draws a gridded vector field —
wind, currents — as particles advected in Go and painted through one batched
opcode. The opcode came first: `3853e1b5` adds `paintSegments`, one segment per
index with a colour each and one width for the batch, because the painter lane
had a batched marker and rect but only one line at a time, which holds for a
graph's edges and not for tens of thousands of trail segments a frame. The host
draws the batch as one untextured mesh; `tessellated()` asks for an epaint line
per segment instead, so the two can be measured against each other without a
rebuild.

`2bac117f` is the source contract: `vectorfield.SourceI` answers a window —
bounds, a step, a ceiling on columns and rows — whose size is bounded by the
request, never by the data, with a missing step an error rather than an empty
window. Its first implementation is an in-memory pyramid that halves by a
coverage-weighted vector mean and carries the scalar mean of the magnitude
beside it, since the vector mean is short wherever directions disagree and
colour should not be; `vectorfieldtest.Run` is the conformance suite, which
checks the contract's form and says it cannot check a source's meteorology.
`28c24703` is the layer, `portolan/flowoverlay`: a map guest that fetches a
window slightly larger than the view off the frame goroutine, resamples it once
into projected world units so a tick carries no trigonometry, advances
float64 particles on a fixed tick by the midpoint rule, and paints every trail
in one `paintSegments`. Its package doc leads with what the picture does not
say — direction and relative speed, not transport; a streamlet, not a
trajectory. `d8f3a25b` puts it in the gallery over an analytic jet with a scene
that asserts one opcode, one request for the first view and no request for a
pan inside the margin. The how-to is
[vector-field-on-a-map](../howto/vector-field-on-a-map.md) (`5c5d4a1d`).

M5 is a trial. `c2418658` adds the arm the ADR decided against — one
`paintLine` per segment — and a `flowbench` demo to measure it, and
[flow-particles-frame-cost](../trials/flow-particles-frame-cost/README.md)
(`961d98c8`, `dd9472e4`, `4128cf1e`) records the result. Its §0, on one
machine: the per-line arm costs the Go side about 1 µs per segment, 42–47 ms a
frame at 5 000 particles, against 3.6–5 ms for the shipped `paintSegments` arm
on the same frame. The per-line figure is the arm that exists only to be
measured, not the layer's cost. The trial's first run found most of the
batched arm's Go side to be FFFI slice marshalling one element per write;
`7d4809e9` writes a slice's elements in one call, which every slice-carrying
opcode — markers, polylines, rects, images — pays less for.

[ADR-0250](../adr/0250-a-sql-backed-vector-field-source-and-plays-vector-field-pane.md)
(accepted; proposed in `11f1f24d`, accepted at `200a929c`) keeps the
field in ClickHouse. `0ab179a2` adds `sqlfield`, a `SourceI` whose every window
is one aggregation binned on the native grid's index, with fixed statement text
per relation and every request value an `ff_` parameter; the describe step
refuses what a mean would hide — more rows per step than grid nodes, nodes off a
regular grid. The integration lane compares its windows sample for sample with
the in-memory pyramid and reads `EXPLAIN` to see a regional window prune on the
primary key. `9bba2392` is play's Vector field pane over a `vector_field` CTE,
whose schema is read under `LIMIT 0` so a missing column is reported before a
row reaches the client; `vf_t` and the view bounds are reserved signals.
`f70e1d88` gives the pane the progress readout a lane-owning panel has — five
statements run between a Run and the first particle, and a status line that
said one sentence read as a stalled pane — and `7e935cc8` adds an optional
`vector_field_sites` CTE drawing the stations a field was measured at, because
a field interpolated from scattered stations renders as smoothly three hundred
kilometres from a measurement as on top of one, and only the query's author can
say where the stations were. `206ce7d0` (ADR-0250 and ADR-0249 Updates
2026-09-28) adds a legend row: colour is speed, pace is speed clamped between
two speeds the layer reports, and the trail count is a display setting, not
data. The how-to gains the ClickHouse-resident form (`4681a5d9`).

## A time strip for stepped series (ADR-0251)

[ADR-0251](../adr/0251-a-time-strip-for-stepped-series.md) (**proposed**,
`cd407c63`) replaces the step slider and play checkbox both flow hosts had. The
slider said nothing about which steps were worth visiting, what a move would
cost against a database, or that steps are unevenly spaced (hourly, then
three-hourly), and playback advanced on the clock whether or not the next
window had arrived. `9ffa9dbc` adds `timescrubber`: steps at their own instants
on a calendar axis, a notch per step for its load state, a bar and a peak stem,
a range band and a clock-free `Transport` value that enters a bracket only when
both ends are ready. The layer side is `dd7f8325` (`StepState`, what the layer
holds of a step) and the data side `deb0105f` (`sqlfield.SummarizeE`, each
step's mean and maximum speed in the view in one statement, the mean weighted
by the cosine of latitude so the polar rows of a lat/lon grid do not outvote
the rest). `c09503d1` and `62662641` put the strip under the gallery demo and
play's pane.

A [survey of other time controls](../adr-background-work/time-scrubber-survey.md)
(`1fc44975`) then drove two passes. `338f7369` adds dwell at the ends (without

starts, an `Ahead` list of the steps playback reaches next — property-tested
against `Advance` so a loader and the playhead cannot disagree — and range
editing operations. `f04e5b9d` (SD6–SD10) repairs a double click that cleared
the range and also stopped playback, keeps the playhead's instant rather than
its index across a reload, states load by shape rather than hue since the
colours are the data's, and tints the past for a forecast. `1d02c261` makes
the layer hold the steps `Ahead` lists — at most eight — which takes up the
`PrefetchE` deferral of ADR-0250 from the demand side, and `ca4681d8` has play's

## swisstopo coordinates as SQL UDFs (ADR-0244)

[ADR-0244](../adr/0244-swisstopo-coordinate-udfs.md) (accepted) ships the
LV95 ⇄ WGS84 transforms a second time, as a pure-SQL family under `SWISSTOPO_`
(`a8a826ab`), because the Go-only version forced a query to leave the server
and give up the predicate, join or aggregate beside the data, and ClickHouse's
own geo functions all assume WGS84 input. The plain name is the truncated
series (~1 m) and `_EXACT` the closed form (~1.5 cm against REFRAME). Three
spellings are forced and commented where they appear: `atanh`/`sinh` instead of
`ln(tan(…))` because the engine's `exp`/`log` carry ~1e-9 relative error
(~5 mm on the ground); a one-element `arrayMap` as a let-binding because UDF
parameters substitute textually and the exact chain otherwise overflows
`max_expanded_ast_elements`; and a closing `CAST` for named tuple elements,
which costs Nullable arguments. Nothing installs the family; the accessors hand
the script to a caller that holds a client.

## rutter: road routing on customizable contraction hierarchies (ADR-0256)

[ADR-0256](../adr/0256-rutter-road-routing-engine-customizable-contraction-hierarchies.md)
(accepted, `dc08eeaa`) takes up ADR-0229 §SD7's deferral of weighted shortest
paths, for a downstream consumer's foot and bike routing over the Swiss road
layer with a cost profile that changes per question. A plain Contraction
Hierarchy bakes the metric into its order, so each profile or closure is a
rebuild; a CCH computes a metric-independent order once and customizes a metric
cheaply. No pure-Go implementation existed. `17ec6f74` lands M1–M2: a routing
graph that keeps parallel arcs, Dijkstra with a 4-ary heap, the Inertial Flow
nested-dissection order, the CCH with customization, elimination-tree queries
and path unpacking, bucket many-to-many and a grid index for snapping — the
lane holds every hierarchy answer equal to Dijkstra's over random grids.
`56b99b74` stores polyline coordinates in float32 (a quarter-metre at a
country's extent in metres) and adds `NearestWhere`, so a point snapped under a
profile lands on a road the profile can use. `feafad3f` adds Newson–Krumm map
matching — an HMM over candidate polylines decoded by Viterbi, breaking where an
observation has no candidate — which moved out of Deferred (`fe822ddc`).
`b820433a` records first country-size measurements as a dated update; they are
the ADR's, not a trial's.

## Reading ClickHouse results: chrows (ADR-0257)

[ADR-0257](../adr/0257-clickhouse-arrow-results-into-column-structs.md)
(accepted) gives the tree one reader of ClickHouse Arrow results (`d06b528a`).
Two costs repeated from caller to caller: readers wanting Go values asked for

stricter where they wrapped.

## leeway and recordstore: declarations, views, generated startup

[ADR-0233](../adr/0233-leeway-params-codec-declaration.md) (accepted) lets a
section state how its membership params blobs are encoded (`6a0a9eda`). The
params channel is opaque bytes, and a convention without a declaration kept
producing divergent writers — the latest, outside this tree, spelled an index
`%04d`, identical to the codec for ten occurrences and wrong after. The
declaration rides ADR-0213's road as a section use-aspect in an exclusive
`params-codec` family; a declaration without a params-bearing channel is a
schema error, and undeclared schemas regenerate byte-identically. No generator
consults it yet; that waits on a second codec with a named consumer.

`e0e0f641` adds `ComposeCreateView` (ADR-0102 Update 2026-09-18): a derived
leeway table — a per-kind cut, a bridge between layouts — has no writer, so it
is a view, and until now spelled every physical column by hand. Each expression
is cast to the table's declared type, a missing one fails the composition, and
view-level `SETTINGS` travel with it. In recordstore, `ed1ad912` generates
`Open<Store>` (construct, ensure, verify) and `FlushIfDue`, both hand-written
beside generated stores in several places, and `79d0436c` adds
`ScanLive<Kind>` and a key-prefix scan, told under app state above (ADR-0100
updates).

`2b85d5c8` makes the `LW_ID_HAS_TAG` UDF prune the primary key: its body derives
the tag's comma bit and code with scalar arithmetic only, so a literal tag folds
server-side and `KeyCondition` prunes as on the macro's `BETWEEN`, where any
lambda in that arithmetic is never folded and reads every granule. The
general explanation is
[clickhouse-udf-primary-key-pruning](../explanation/clickhouse-udf-primary-key-pruning.md);
`adc698e7` drops the decoder's per-row array.

## Tooling: Rust licences (ADR-0246), fuzz and benchmark lanes

[ADR-0246](../adr/0246-license-gate-rust-crate-trees.md) (accepted, `2ced9953`)
points the licence gate at Cargo (`f83c4441`). The gate read one identifier per
row, and a crate declares an SPDX expression; splitting one errs both ways —
`MIT OR LGPL-2.1-or-later` fails a crate that offers MIT, and
`<unknown> AND GPL-3.0-only` leaves the unknown out of the verdict.
`EvaluateExpressionE` keeps the structure: OR elects its most permissive known
branch, AND is bound by its most restrictive, and an unclassifiable identifier
degrades to advisory, never to a pass. The CSV appends two columns, so readers
of the original four are unaffected; the Go path is pinned unchanged.

`29179adb` adds a fuzz lane, since `go test -fuzz` takes one target per
invocation: every target runs for `BOXER_FUZZ_TIME`, a failure does not stop
the lane, and failing inputs are listed for committing as regression seeds.
`00c9128c` adds a benchmark lane: the default runner compiles benchmarks and
never runs them, so `smoke` runs each once and lists skips with their reasons,
and `compare` alternates base-ref and working-tree binaries under benchstat,
declared as a go tool at the version already in the module graph. `62f06881`
lets `chlocalpool` find clickhouse on `PATH` or via `BOXER_CLICKHOUSE_LOCAL`
when no path is configured; every in-process endpoint over the pool had refused
to start on a single-binary or user-local install (ADR-0028 Update).

  the JSONL form came back as a flag in `1c3fd5a6`.
- **the browser tab's Node server.** Grown with the wasm spike and retired by
  `55c520b5`, which serves the bundle from Go.
- **the widget conformance allowlist and the deprecated `Events()` accessors.**
  Both were scaffolding for the ADR-0267 migration. The allowlist of twelve
  packages arrived in `9e6e40b1` and was emptied by `404b534e`; the accessors
  were kept by M1 and deleted by M6 (`7c646cbe`).
