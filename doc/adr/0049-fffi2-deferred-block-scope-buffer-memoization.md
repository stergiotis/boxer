# ADR-0049: Per-call-site buffer-size memoization for FFFI2 deferred-block scopes

## Context

### What `DeferredBlockScope` is and why it allocates

[`public/thestack/fffi2/runtime/fffi2_rt_deferred.go`](../../public/thestack/fffi2/runtime/fffi2_rt_deferred.go) implements the per-frame capture buffer that backs every widget primitive whose body is emitted out of order from its header — `c.EndETable` (cells + headers), `c.DockArea` (per-tab bodies), `c.Window`/`c.Tooltip` (tip + target), `c.NodeDir` (node body), and so on. Each `DeferredBlockScope` owns two `*bytes.Buffer`:

- `dataBuf` — slab holding all block keys plus the captured opcode payload, constructed via `bytes.NewBuffer(make([]byte, 0, 64*1024))` (`fffi2_rt_deferred.go:77`)
- `tempBuf` — capture target redirected from `Fffi2.SendIntermediate` between `Begin()` / `End()`, constructed via `bytes.NewBuffer(make([]byte, 0, 4096))` (`fffi2_rt_deferred.go:78`)

Construction is generated by `fffi2/compiletime/goserver/fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go:624` into `bindings/factories.out.go` — one scope per `deferred*` field on every Fluid builder type, instantiated **per widget call**. The Fluid is short-lived: it lives from `c.X(...)` until `et.Send()`, where `SpliceDeferredBlockMap(scope)` copies the captured bytes into the `RetainedFffiBuilder`'s buffer and the scope falls out of reference. The OS pipe handles writing the assembled frame to the Rust client; the scope itself becomes GC garbage.

### What pprof says about the cost

Bounded delta-alloc capture over a 30 s window (`pprof -base t1 t2`, on the demo carousel showing the imztop subcommand with the ECDF inspector closed):

| Source | Cum / 30 s | Share |
|---|---|---|
| `bytes.growSlice` (called from `bytes.(*Buffer).grow`) | **12,841 MB** | **87.0 %** |
| `imztop.renderOneCPUSparkline-range1` | 2,850 MB | 19.3 % |
| `imztop.procCellLabel` | 421 MB | 2.9 % |
| `proc.(*Collector).collectAll` (post-fix) | 371 MB | 2.5 % |
| Everything else | — | <2 % |

Total ≈ 14.7 GB / 30 s ≈ 490 MB/s. Decomposing `bytes.growSlice` callers with `pprof -peek 'bytes.(*Buffer).grow'`:

| Caller of `Buffer.Write → grow` | Share | Buffer grown |
|---|---|---|
| `DeferredBlockScope.End` | 31 % | scope `dataBuf` (slab append of `tempBuf` contents) |
| `DeferredBlockScope.WriteToFixedKey` | 41 % | caller's `*bytes.Buffer` (i.e. `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf`) |
| `Fffi2.SendIntermediate` | 16 % | bufio writer / channel |
| `DeferredBlockScope.AppendRawToCapture` | 10 % | scope `tempBuf` |
| `DeferredBlockScope.Begin` | 1 % | scope `dataBuf` (key bytes) |

Scope-internal growth (`End` + `AppendRawToCapture` + `Begin`) — the bucket this ADR addresses — is ~42 % of `growSlice`, i.e. **~5.4 GB / 30 s ≈ 180 MB/s** of churn. The remaining 41 % through `WriteToFixedKey` grows the *consumer* `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf`, which is pooled separately at `fffi2/typed/fffi2_typed_impl.go:48` but with a 4 KiB cap (`largestPooledBuffer`) that excludes virtually every real-world buffer from reuse — a related issue, but out of scope here.

### Why we measure cost too, not just allocate

The original presenting symptom was a visible imztop stutter. After fixing the proc-collector heap pressure (35 GB → 372 MB per 30 s in `proc.(*Collector).collectAll`, see the scratch-buffer refactor in [`public/observability/sysmetrics/proc/proc.go`](../../public/observability/sysmetrics/proc/proc.go)), the visible stutter persisted. Slow-frame instrumentation at [`public/thestack/imzero2/metrics/metrics.go`](../../public/thestack/imzero2/metrics/metrics.go) showed `sync_us` ≈ 25-32 ms on 5-10 % of frames, with `written_b` ≈ 250-330 KB per frame consistently across all frames (slow and fast). The stutter source turned out to be Rust paint occasionally overrunning the 16.6 ms vsync deadline — not heap pressure on the Go side.

That diagnosis taught us something the existing metrics don't surface: **per-widget-primitive wire-byte cost is not directly observable today**. We had to infer it by elimination from the slow-frame logger and grep through alloc profiles. A measurement primitive that reports "the cells scope cost 280 KB this frame; the tooltip target cost 0.5 KB" would have shortcut the entire investigation.

### Forces

- **Allocation pressure is real but not the dominant stutter cause.** Cutting `growSlice` is worth doing, but the design choice should not be ranked primarily on raw allocation bytes saved. Both candidate solutions kill `growSlice` for the addressed surface.
- **Observability of widget cost has zero existing surface.** Frame-level wire bytes are recorded (`metrics.FrameMetrics.LastWritten`); per-primitive bytes are not. Adding instrumentation in widget code is a slippery slope — it scales with widget count, requires constant discipline, and creates a second debug-only code path.
- **`sync.Pool` carries operational hazards.** Go #23199 (oversize buffers pinned indefinitely), per-P shard quirks, victim-cache GC semantics, and the Release-correctness invariant (must put back exactly once, must not touch after Put). All known, all manageable, all extra cognitive load.
- **Codegen is already the seam.** `fffi2/compiletime/goserver/fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go` emits the scope-construction line and the Send-method body that consumes the scope. Whatever sizing-aware behavior we introduce must be expressible as one extra emitter slot.
- **The build must stay CGO-free** (ADR-0026 invariant) and must not introduce a mandatory new codegen step beyond `./generate.sh`.

## Design space (QOC)

**Question.** How should `DeferredBlockScope` allocate its `dataBuf` and `tempBuf` to (a) eliminate the per-frame `bytes.growSlice` traffic and (b) make the per-primitive wire cost observable, without taking on hazards disproportionate to the wins?

**Options.**

- **O1 — sync.Pool the buffers.** Pool `*bytes.Buffer` instances; `NewDeferredBlockScope` `Get()`s, codegen emits `Release()` calls after `SpliceDeferredBlockMap` that `Put()` them back. Apply a per-pool oversize cap to mitigate Go #23199.
- **O2 — Per-call-site memoization (this ADR).** Each scope kind (`cells`, `headers`, `tabBody`, `nodeBody`, `tip`, `target`, `rows`) gets an `atomic.Uint64` hint. `NewDeferredBlockScope` allocates `make([]byte, 0, hint)`; on Release the scope folds its observed high-water mark back into the hint via a CAS loop with slow exponential decay. Side effect: the hint table is a permanent zero-cost cost-attribution surface available to overlays, tests, and HTTP endpoints.
- **O3 — Hybrid: pool + memoize.** Buffers come from a per-kind pool keyed on size class; misses fall back to a memoized initial allocation. Pool hits give zero-alloc reuse; misses give first-try right-sized allocation.
- **O4 — Do nothing.** Accept the 180 MB/s scope-buffer churn; it's not the visible stutter source.

**Criteria.**

- **C1 — Allocation traffic reduction** (measured against the 5.4 GB / 30 s scope-internal `growSlice` budget).
- **C2 — Observability bonus.** Does the design produce a re-usable measurement of per-widget-primitive wire cost?
- **C3 — Operational simplicity.** Number of failure modes, depth of test surface, behavior under panics, behavior under one-off oversize frames.
- **C4 — Codegen-template footprint.** How many emitter slots in `fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go` change, how invasive the change to `methods.out.go`.
- **C5 — Compatibility with the future `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf` pooling work.** Whether this design composes with or blocks a follow-up that fixes the 41 % `WriteToFixedKey` share.

|    | O1 (sync.Pool) | O2 (memoize) | O3 (hybrid) | O4 (do nothing) |
|----|----|----|----|----|
| **C1** allocation savings | ++ (eliminates base alloc too) | + (eliminates growth; base alloc remains) | ++ (best of both) | −− |
| **C2** observability bonus | −− (none) | ++ (per-kind cost gauge) | + (memoized hint still exists in the hybrid) | −− |
| **C3** operational simplicity | − (oversize cap, Release-correctness, per-P quirks) | + (one atomic CAS, no pool to misuse) | −− (every hazard of O1, plus a tunable interaction with the hint) | ++ |
| **C4** codegen footprint | + (one new emitter slot) | + (one new emitter slot + hint var per kind) | − (two emitter slots, two storages) | ++ |
| **C5** composition w/ future builder-pool work | + (orthogonal) | ++ (hint feeds builder-pool too) | + (orthogonal) | n/a |

The dominating axis is **C2**. O1 and O4 produce no measurement surface; O3's measurement comes from the same memoization layer O2 introduces. O2 leaves ~80-100 MB/s on the table compared to O1 (the per-scope base allocation), but recovers all of it in surface area: the hint table is an observability primitive future work can build on (frame-cost histograms, per-kind budgets, CI regression checks, adaptive UI), where the pool produces only the allocation reduction.

## Decision

We will introduce **per-call-site memoization of buffer-size hints** as the sizing strategy for `DeferredBlockScope.dataBuf` and `DeferredBlockScope.tempBuf`, and treat the hint table as a first-class observability surface.

Concretely:

1. **One `atomic.Uint64` per scope kind**, declared in a generated companion file. Scope kinds are taken from the IDL `deferredBlockMap` names that already exist in `fffi2/compiletime/goserver/fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go:624`'s emitter (`cells`, `headers`, `tabBody`, `nodeBody`, `tip`, `target`, `rows`, …):

   ```go
   // public/thestack/imzero2/egui2/bindings/deferredhints.out.go — generated
   package bindings

   import "sync/atomic"

   var (
       hintCells       atomic.Uint64
       hintHeaders     atomic.Uint64
       hintTabBody     atomic.Uint64
       hintNodeBody    atomic.Uint64
       hintTip         atomic.Uint64
       hintTarget      atomic.Uint64
       hintRows        atomic.Uint64
   )
   ```

2. **`NewDeferredBlockScopeHinted` constructor variant** in `public/thestack/fffi2/runtime/fffi2_rt_deferred.go` that takes a `dataHint *atomic.Uint64` and pre-sizes `dataBuf` from it (with a floor — `deferredDataBufFloor = 4 KiB` — to keep tiny scopes from triggering re-grow on their first byte). The legacy `NewDeferredBlockScope` remains as a thin shim wrapping a process-static hint, for non-codegen callers.

3. **`(*DeferredBlockScope).ReleaseWithHint()` method** that folds the observed `dataBuf.Len()` back into the hint via a CAS loop with peak-and-slow-decay update rule:

   ```
   if observed >= old: next = observed                          // ratchet up
   else:               next = old - ((old - observed) >> 5)     // 1/32 step toward observed
   ```

   The decay shift (5) gives a ~30-frame half-life — fast enough to recover from a transient spike within ~half a second at 60 Hz, slow enough that frame-to-frame jitter does not destabilise the hint into oscillation. The Release also drops references to `dataBuf`, `tempBuf`, `entries`, and `getFffi` so the buffers GC.

4. **Codegen emits the `ReleaseWithHint()` call** after the last `SpliceDeferredBlockMap` for each scope in every generated `Send()` method. The natural emitter slot is the same one that emits the existing `r.SpliceDeferredBlockMap(inst.deferredCells)` line at `methods.out.go:1220` and siblings.

5. **`ScopeHintsSnapshot()` API** in `public/thestack/fffi2/runtime/` returning a `[]ScopeHintSnapshot` (kind name, observed high-water bytes). Wire it into:
   - **`/debug/pprof/custom/scopehints`** HTTP handler under the existing pprof listener (`--pprofHttpListenAddress`).
   - **The slow-frame logger** at `public/thestack/imzero2/metrics/metrics.go`'s `RecordBytes`: when a frame exceeds `SlowFrameThresholdNs`, log the top-N scope kinds by current hint alongside the existing render/sync/interpret/written/read breakdown.
   - **The metricsoverlay widget** (`public/thestack/imzero2/egui2/widgets/metricsoverlay/metricsoverlay.go`) as an opt-in column behind an env-toggle, so the dev demo can read per-kind cost without rebuilding.

Codegen change footprint, in lines, against `fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go`:

- One new function emitting `deferredhints.out.go` (declares the per-kind atomic vars). Run once at codegen time, indexed by the IDL `deferredBlockMap` names.
- One modification to the per-scope construction line at line 624 to thread the hint pointer.
- One modification to the per-Send-method emitter to append `inst.deferredX.ReleaseWithHint()` after each `r.SpliceDeferredBlockMap(inst.deferredX)`.

## Alternatives

- **O1 — sync.Pool the buffers.** Rejected primarily on **C2 (observability bonus)** rather than on its allocation merits, which are strictly stronger than O2 by ~80-100 MB/s on the base-buffer path. A pool produces only allocation savings; this ADR's design produces allocation savings *and* a permanent passive cost-attribution surface that future work (CI regression tests, frame-cost overlays, adaptive UI, payload-budget planning) can build on without re-instrumentation. O1's operational hazards (oversize cap, Release-correctness invariant, sync.Pool's per-P + victim-cache GC semantics) compound the design cost. If the post-implementation measurement shows the base-buffer allocation is still load-bearing, we revisit by layering a pool *under* the memoization (the hybrid in O3) as a Tier-2 ADR update, not a supersession.
- **O3 — Hybrid: pool + memoize.** Rejected as premature. It inherits every hazard of O1 while only marginally improving on O2's allocation savings. Reserve as a follow-up after the memoization design is proven in steady-state.
- **O4 — Do nothing.** Rejected because the 180 MB/s of `growSlice` is the second-largest allocator in the system after the (already-fixed) proc collector, and the design's actual headline value — observability — is not available from any alternative path without a separate instrumentation effort.

## Consequences

### Positive

- **`bytes.growSlice` traffic from `DeferredBlockScope` drops to zero in steady state** after the hint stabilises (~30 frames per kind under the 1/32 decay shift). At 5.4 GB / 30 s budget that's ~180 MB/s removed from `growSlice` cum. The base `make([]byte, 0, hint)` cost — one per scope per frame — remains; with hint stability this is roughly the same total bytes as the eventual buffer would have grown to, but allocated in one shot rather than as a series of doublings.
- **Per-widget-primitive wire cost becomes a first-class, passive observable.** No application code changes are needed to gain visibility into which FFFI2 primitive contributes how much to a frame's wire payload. The hint table is updated as a side effect of sizing; observability does not cost extra CPU at steady state beyond the one CAS per Release that the sizing requires anyway.
- **The slow-frame logger gains attribution.** A future log line for a slow frame can include "top contributors: cells=262144 tabBody=15234 rows=8192" alongside the existing breakdown, so triage moves from "Go side or Rust side?" to "*which* Go-side scope drove this frame's payload?" in one step.
- **CI gains a regression bar at no extra runtime cost.** Tests can snapshot a hint at the end of a deterministic scene render and assert it against a budget; a PR that doubles a widget's per-row cost fails at test time, not in production.
- **The decision composes with the future `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf` pool work.** A pooled builder buffer can pre-size from `sum(hints)` at the start of a frame and avoid its own growth chain. The hint table becomes a planner input.

### Negative

- **The base `make([]byte, 0, hint)` cost is not amortised across frames.** Compared to a pool, each scope still allocates one buffer per frame; only the doublings disappear. For a hint-stable scope this is roughly 1 alloc per scope per frame at exactly the right size, which is acceptable. If `sum(hints) × scopes_per_frame × fps` ever climbs above the GC trigger threshold meaningfully, the pool-as-Tier-2-update path is open.
- **Per-kind aggregation, not per-instance.** Two `c.EndETable` instances on screen share `hintCells`; the hint reports the max across all simultaneous cells-using widgets. Fine for budgeting and triage; insufficient for "which specific ETable instance is expensive?". Recoverable by widening the key to `(kind, widget_id)` with a `sync.Map`, which is a larger change deferred to a follow-up ADR.
- **Hint oscillation under pathological workloads.** The 1/32 decay shift is empirical. A workload that toggles a scope's size in a sawtooth pattern faster than the half-life will see the hint hover near the upper envelope, over-allocating relative to the median frame. The decay shift is tunable; if observed in production we move it to a build-tag or env knob rather than re-litigating in code.
- **One additional emitter slot in `fffi2_compiletime_go_server.go`.** Manageable in isolation; cumulative codegen complexity needs a generator-level explanation pass eventually (out of scope here).

### Neutral

- **Replaces an implicit assumption with an explicit hint.** Before: each scope's `64*1024` initial cap is hard-coded at `fffi2_rt_deferred.go:77`. After: the cap is a per-kind hint with an explicit update rule. Tunability moves from "edit the constant" to "tune the decay shift or the floor". Net code complexity is comparable; the locus of behavior changes.
- **The hint table grows with the number of IDL deferred-block kinds.** Each new IDL deferred block adds one `atomic.Uint64` (8 bytes) and one `ScopeHintSnapshot` entry. Negligible.
- **Process restart resets all hints to zero.** The first frame after start allocates from the floor (4 KiB) and grows on the cold path; the cost of that first frame's growth is bounded and not perceptibly different from cold-start under the current design.

## Updates

### 2026-08-18 — The `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf` follow-up (C5), resolved

Context flagged the 41 % `WriteToFixedKey` share as growing the *consumer* `RetainedFffiBuilder.buf`, "pooled separately … with a 4 KiB cap (`largestPooledBuffer`) that excludes virtually every real-world buffer from reuse — a related issue, but out of scope here"; C5 scored the options on composing with that follow-up. It was measured again at HEAD and is now resolved.

Measured on the demo carousel hosting `play` (Table tab) via `--pprofHttpListenAddress`:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `bytes.growSlice` share of all bytes allocated | 47.8 % (26.5 GB of 54 GB) |
| mean `growSlice` allocation | 31.4 KiB over 844,904 allocations — 7.7× the 4 KiB ceiling |
| callers of `Buffer.Write → grow` | `WriteToFixedKey` 47.5 %, `DeferredBlockScope.End` 23.4 %, `Fffi2.SendIntermediate` 15.6 %, `AppendRawToCapture` 8.4 % |
| wire bytes per frame | ~460 KiB (the Rust side independently reports ~489 KiB of scope data per frame) |
| pool *hit* rate | ~99.9 % |

The ceiling inverted the pool's intent. It was never missing — it only ever handed back buffers ≤4 KiB, so the buffers cheap to grow were the only ones retained and every buffer expensive to grow was discarded and re-doubled from `defaultBufferSize` on the next frame. Against a small live heap (89 MB, default `GOGC=100`, no `GOMEMLIMIT`) that drove roughly 6–10 collections per second; `runtime.gcDrain` was 37.8 % cum of a render-busy CPU profile.

**Decision.** `largestPooledBuffer` goes 4 KiB → 256 KiB, and `defaultBufferSize` is decoupled from it — it was `largestPooledBuffer / 8`, so raising the ceiling alone would have silently taken every fresh buffer from 512 B to 32 KiB; it is now an independent 512 B. Ceiling-only: above the working set the pool is self-tuning, because grown buffers survive `Put` and `Get` returns a right-sized buffer within a few frames, so no sizing logic is needed. Retention stays bounded by `sync.Pool`'s collector-driven clearing (victim cache, two GC cycles), which is what golang/go#23199 asks for.

Rejected: **size-bucketed pools** — a bucket-selection policy and its test surface, for a win plain reuse already delivers; **extending this ADR's per-kind hint to the builder** (C5's `++`) — it sizes only the first frame, and reuse covers steady state.

**Verification.** `TestPoolRetainsRealisticFrameBuffer` pins the predicate rather than the outcome: a 128 KiB buffer must still satisfy the retention test. It fails at the old ceiling (`131072 is not less than or equal to 4096`). Pool identity is deliberately not asserted — `sync.Pool` may drop entries at any GC, so a `Put`/`Get` round-trip is not a testable property.

**Still open from this ADR.** Decision item 5's `/debug/pprof/custom/scopehints` handler was never wired; of the three named consumers only the slow-frame logger landed (`metrics.go`'s `RecordBytes`). `ScopeHintsSnapshot` is exported and unused elsewhere, so the endpoint remains a small follow-up.

### 2026-08-19 — Verified in the app: `WriteToFixedKey` growth is down 97 %

The entry above changed the ceiling on the strength of a unit test and arithmetic. Measured since, on the real render path.

Method: both hosts built from one pristine detached worktree at the same commit, differing only in `largestPooledBuffer`, so nothing else in the tree moves between arms. Scene is `play`'s Table tab over a twelve-column result set (`BOXER_PLAY_SQL` + `AUTORUN` + `FOCUS_TABLE`), rendered into a headless weston at 60 Hz. Allocation is a delta of two `/debug/pprof/allocs` captures across a 30 s window; both arms rendered **3 602 frames**, counted from `wl_surface.commit`, so the comparison is normalised by work rather than by time.

| Metric (30 s, 3 602 frames) | 4 KiB ceiling | 256 KiB ceiling | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total allocated | 1 313 MB | 1 065 MB | −19 % |
| `bytes.growSlice` | 364 MB | 144 MB | −60 % |
| — of which `WriteToFixedKey` | 203 MB | 6.6 MB | **−97 %** |
| `growSlice` per frame | 101 KiB | 40 KiB | −61 KiB |
| Live heap at end of run | 60.6 MB | 49.3 MB | −19 % |

The site the ceiling governs is essentially gone, and the drop in total allocation is accounted for by it. Live heap fell rather than rose, so the retention that motivated the original 4 KiB cap (golang/go#23199) did not materialise here — fewer large short-lived buffers are in flight at any instant.

What remains under `growSlice` is a different buffer and outside this ADR: `Fffi2.SendIntermediate` (61 → 64 MB, untouched, as expected) and `DeferredBlockScope.End` (25 → 20 MB), the latter being the `dataBuf` this ADR's hint already governs. `SendIntermediate` is now the largest single `growSlice` caller and has had no sizing work at all — the natural next candidate, on the same evidence this entry used.

### 2026-08-19 — Scopes are pooled; the C1 gap this ADR left open is closed

Consequences recorded the cost this design accepted: "the base `make([]byte, 0, hint)` cost is not amortised across frames… if `sum(hints) × scopes_per_frame × fps` ever climbs above the GC trigger threshold meaningfully, the pool-as-Tier-2-update path is open." Measured on the scene above it had reached **158 MB per 30 s**, larger than all remaining `growSlice` traffic combined and the biggest single FFFI2 allocation site. Taking the path.

Each `ScopeHint` now carries a `sync.Pool` beside its atomic. `ReleaseWithHint` — already the deterministic release point every generated `Send()` calls — empties and recycles the scope rather than nil'ing it. The two halves are complementary rather than redundant: the hint sizes a *cold* buffer (start-up, or after a collection has drained the pool), the pool removes the per-frame allocation for every scope after that.

Same protocol as the entry above — both arms from one pristine worktree at one commit, 3 602 identical frames:

| Metric (30 s) | Per-frame alloc | Pooled | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope construction | 158 MB | 7 MB | **−95 %** |
| `bytes.growSlice` | 142 MB | 90 MB | −36 % |
| — of which `DeferredBlockScope.End` | 20 MB | 0.5 MB | −97 % |
| Total allocated | 1 065 MB | 855 MB | −20 % |

`RegisterScopeHint` returns `*ScopeHint` instead of `*atomic.Uint64`, and `NewDeferredBlockScopeHinted` takes the same type — the generated bindings pass one straight to the other, so they compile unchanged and no regeneration is needed.

**Two things this entry deliberately did *not* ship.** Pooling turns use-after-release from a nil-pointer panic into silent cross-frame corruption, so `Begin` and `End` now check a `released` flag and panic loudly; that is a real hazard the previous nil-out shape handled for free. And the M1 note on `deferredTempBufInitialCap` said to "promote to a hint in a follow-up if profiling shows it's worth it" — a `tempHint` was built, tested and measured, moved nothing, and was reverted. Profiling answered the ADR's own question in the negative.

**Where the remaining `growSlice` actually lives.** The previous entry named `Fffi2.SendIntermediate` as the next candidate and assumed it grew a scope's `tempBuf`. It does not. `DockAreaFluid.Tab` ([`egui2_methods.go`](../../public/thestack/imzero2/egui2/bindings/egui2_methods.go), hand-written) captures each tab body into its own `&bytes.Buffer{}` — zero capacity, one per tab per frame — which never touches a scope at all. That is 73 MB per 30 s over ~22 doublings per frame, and it is why neither pooling nor a `tempHint` moved it. It is also not a hint's job: one shared hint would size every tab to the largest tab, allocating more than the doubling it saves. Pooling those buffers is the shape that fits, and it needs a release point after `Send` consumes `inst.bodies`, which alias them.
